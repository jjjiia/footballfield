I started thinking differently about how to understand scale when my son asked how many seconds it was until the weekend. The length of one second was a tangible thing to a child, equivalent to a tick tock sound he can make on his own.

Making use of colloquial and biological units in digital measurements and maps is one way for us to understand things that are impossibly large(like the number atoms in a grain of sand) and constantly growing still(like the wealth of the wealthiest people).

There is a long tradition of measuring things with ones’ own body, at arms lengths, by foot, a hair, a nose, a nail, a step. We can also measure things using what we already know first hand, the size of our living rooms, the length of our commutes, the cost of our labor, the number of people in this room.
